Best eSIMs for Nigerians Travelling Abroad (and How to Avoid Roaming Bills)

By Udochukwu · Promo Radar NG · 19 October 2026

Land in London, Dubai or Accra, switch on your phone, and your Nigerian line starts eating airtime on roaming. Many travellers come home to empty balances or data that never worked at all. A travel eSIM is one of the simplest ways to stay online abroad without paying roaming prices.

1. What is an eSIM?

An eSIM is a digital SIM built into your phone. Instead of buying a plastic SIM card at the airport, you buy a data plan online, scan a QR code or tap "install", and your phone connects to a local network when you land.

The big advantage: you can keep your Nigerian SIM in the phone for calls, bank OTPs and WhatsApp, while the eSIM handles your data.

2. Check your phone first

Not every phone supports eSIM. Before you buy anything, check two things:

  • eSIM support: Most iPhones from the XS/XR onwards, recent Samsung Galaxy S and Z models, and Google Pixel phones support it. Many budget Android phones sold in Nigeria do not. On iPhone, look for "Add eSIM" under Settings › Mobile Data. On Android, look under Settings › Connections › SIM manager.
  • Unlocked phone: Your phone must not be locked to one network. Phones bought abroad on contract are sometimes locked.
Quick test: Dial *#06#. If you see an "EID" number, your phone supports eSIM.

3. Popular travel eSIM providers

Several international companies sell data-only eSIMs for travellers. Well-known names include Airalo, Holafly, Nomad, Saily and Yesim. They sell plans by country (for example, UK only), by region (Europe, Middle East) or worldwide.

4. Install it before you fly

Buy and install your eSIM while you're still on good Wi-Fi in Nigeria. Most plans only start counting days when you first connect in the destination country. Then on arrival:

  • Turn on the eSIM line for mobile data
  • Turn on data roaming for the eSIM only (this is normal for travel eSIMs)
  • Turn off data roaming on your Nigerian line so it doesn't charge you

5. Keep your Nigerian number working

Your bank OTPs and verification codes still come to your Nigerian line by SMS. Keep that line switched on for calls and texts. Receiving SMS while roaming is often free or cheap, but check with MTN, Airtel, Glo or 9mobile before you travel.

WhatsApp keeps working on your Nigerian number even when your data comes from the eSIM, so your contacts won't notice any change.

6. eSIM vs buying a local SIM

A local SIM bought at the airport can be cheaper for long stays, especially in the UK where monthly plans are good value. For short trips, an eSIM saves time and avoids queues. In some countries, buying a local SIM also requires registration with your passport.

Quick checklist

  • ✅ Confirm your phone supports eSIM (dial *#06# and look for EID)
  • ✅ Make sure the phone is unlocked
  • ✅ Compare plans for your destination and number of days
  • ✅ Install before you leave Nigeria
  • ✅ Switch off roaming on your Nigerian line
  • ✅ Keep your Nigerian SIM on for bank OTPs
