Cheapest Flights from the USA to Lagos and Abuja

By Udochukwu · Promo Radar NG · 19 October 2026

Whether you're in Houston, Atlanta, New York, Washington DC or Chicago, flying home to Nigeria is rarely cheap. It's a long trip with limited nonstop options, and fares jump every December. But Nigerians in America who plan smartly regularly save hundreds of dollars per ticket. Here's how.

1. Nonstop vs one-stop: know your options

Nonstop flights between the US and Lagos have been operated by US airlines such as Delta (from Atlanta) and United (from Washington Dulles). They take roughly 11–12 hours and are the most convenient, but often the most expensive, especially in peak season. Nigerian airlines have also announced plans for US routes, so check what's flying for your dates.

One-stop flights give you far more choice and are often cheaper. Popular connections include:

  • Addis Ababa (Ethiopian Airlines), often one of the cheapest, with good connections to both Lagos and Abuja
  • Istanbul (Turkish Airlines), with many US cities and generous baggage on some fares
  • Doha or Dubai (Qatar Airways, Emirates)
  • Casablanca (Royal Air Maroc) and Cairo (EgyptAir)
  • London, Paris, Amsterdam or Frankfurt (European airlines)
Check before you book: some connections need a transit visa for Nigerian passport holders, for example through the UK or parts of Europe. Exemptions sometimes apply if you hold a valid US visa or green card, but always confirm with the airline or the official embassy website before paying.

2. Lagos or Abuja?

Lagos has the most flights, so it's usually the cheapest. But if your family is in the east or north, compare Abuja too. A slightly dearer ticket into Abuja can still save money once you count a domestic flight or a long road trip from Lagos. Search both, and try multi-city (into one city, out of the other).

For December trips, start watching fares by summer. For other months, two to three months ahead is usually enough. Flying midweek is often cheaper than weekends.

4. Pick the right US airport

Fares can differ a lot between cities. If you live within driving distance of more than one airport (for example in Texas, the Northeast or around DC), compare them. A cheaper flight from a nearby city can be worth a short drive or a cheap domestic hop.

5. Watch the baggage rules

Most people flying home carry gifts, foodstuff and supplies. Check how many checked bags your fare includes. Some airlines on Africa routes allow two bags in economy, while basic fares may allow one or none. Compare the total price including bags, not just the headline fare.

6. Compare, set alerts, then book

  • Compare at least two or three booking sites plus the airline's own website.
  • Set price alerts for your route so you're told when fares drop.
  • Check the latest fares we've found on Nigeria routes in our deals section, updated every few hours.

7. Stay safe

Be careful with "cheap ticket" sellers on WhatsApp and Instagram. After any booking, check your booking reference on the airline's own website using "Manage booking", and pay with a card that offers protection.

Quick checklist

  • ✅ Compare nonstop and one-stop routes
  • ✅ Check transit visa rules for your connection
  • ✅ Search both Lagos and Abuja
  • ✅ Avoid late November to early January if you can
  • ✅ Count baggage in the total price
  • ✅ Verify your booking on the airline's website
