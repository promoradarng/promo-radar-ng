Flight Delayed or Cancelled to or from Nigeria? What You Can Claim

You've waited hours at Heathrow or Murtala Muhammed, the board keeps saying "delayed", and nobody is explaining anything. Many travellers don't know this, but on flights between Nigeria and the UK you may be owed cash compensation of up to £520 per person, plus food and even a hotel. Here's how it works.

Updated 17 October 2026

1. Is your flight covered by UK rules?

The UK has its own passenger-rights law, often called UK261. It covers:

  • Flights leaving the UK, on any airline. For example, London to Lagos or London to Abuja.
  • Flights arriving in the UK only if the airline is a UK or EU airline. For example, Lagos to London on a UK airline is covered, but the same route on a Nigerian or Middle Eastern airline usually is not.
  • Connecting trips on one booking are judged from where the first flight left.

2. How much can you get?

Cash compensation is based on how late you arrive at your final destination. Lagos and Abuja to London are over 3,500 km, so these are the amounts that matter for most of our readers:

  • Arrived 3 to 4 hours late: up to £260 per person
  • Arrived more than 4 hours late: £520 per person
  • Less than 3 hours late: no cash compensation

That's per passenger, so a family of four could be owed over £2,000. It also doesn't matter how cheap your ticket was.

3. When the airline doesn't have to pay

Cash compensation isn't owed if the delay was caused by "extraordinary circumstances" outside the airline's control, such as dangerous weather, air traffic control strikes, airspace closures or security risks.

But routine technical faults and strikes by the airline's own staff generally don't count as extraordinary. Airlines sometimes blame "operational reasons", so always ask for the reason in writing.

4. Food, calls and a hotel, whatever the cause

On long flights like Lagos–London, once you're delayed 4 hours or more at departure, the airline must look after you, even in bad weather:

  • Meals and drinks suitable for the wait
  • Two phone calls or emails
  • A hotel and transport to it, if you're stuck overnight

If they don't provide it, buy reasonable food or a modest hotel yourself and keep every receipt to claim back later.

If the delay reaches 5 hours and you no longer want to travel, you can ask for a refund of the unused ticket.

5. How to claim (you don't need to pay an agency)

  1. Write to the airline that operated the flight (check your boarding pass, as it may differ from the one you booked with). Include your booking reference, flight number, date, every passenger's name, how late you arrived, and receipts.
  2. If they say no, ask why in writing.
  3. Escalate for free: many airlines belong to a UK dispute scheme such as AviationADR or CEDR, whose decision the airline must follow. If your airline isn't in one, contact the UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA).
  4. Small claims court is the last resort.

In England and Wales you generally have up to six years to claim (five in Scotland), so old delays may still count.

Online claim companies can do the work for you, but they take a share of your money. If you're comfortable writing an email, you can keep 100% by claiming directly.

6. Flying within or out of Nigeria?

Nigeria's aviation regulator, the NCAA, also has passenger rules. According to the NCAA, refunds for cancelled or delayed flights should be paid immediately for cash tickets, or within 14 days for card or transfer payments. For delayed bags on international flights, it has said passengers are entitled to a first-needs payment while the airline searches. If an airline ignores you, you can raise a complaint with the NCAA's consumer protection team.

Quick checklist at the airport

  • ✅ Take a photo of the departure board showing the delay
  • ✅ Ask staff for the reason, and later get it in writing
  • ✅ Keep boarding passes and all receipts
  • ✅ Note the exact time you arrive and the doors open
  • ✅ Claim from the operating airline, then escalate if refused
